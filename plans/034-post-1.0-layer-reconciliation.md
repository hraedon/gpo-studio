### How WP-3 surfaces are built: in `api.py`

`policy_families.py`, `security_template.py`, `object_security.py`, `sddl.py`
and the two candidate builders are all in their lanes' bound file sets. A
surface that edits any of them expires a live verdict and costs an estate run.
So surfaces compose in `api.py`, which no lane binds.
`tests/test_policy_family_surface.py` holds the policy-family composition
equal to the certified builder's output in both scopes. `object_security.py`
followed the same route the same day.

### Defects found by building the object-security surface

Building the surface found two defects the lane could not reach:

- **WI-064.** The restricted-groups writer emits `S-1-5-32-544__Members` where
  Windows exports `*S-1-5-32-544__Members`. It stars the SIDs in the entry's
  value but not the one in its key. A tolerant parser makes Studio's own round
  trip come out clean. The candidate has no restricted-groups rows, so the lane
  never saw it.
- **WI-065.** `SystemServicesFamily.validate` reports "could not be parsed" for
  a descriptor nothing tried to parse. Validating the lane's own candidate gives
  three errors for an SDDL that Windows accepted and re-exported byte for byte.
  The candidate builder never calls that path.

Both are filed against the batch that re-runs the estate, because the module is
bound by its verdict. This supports WP-3's ordering: the lane measures the
bytes on the wire, and the surface exercises the rest of the module before an
operator does.

## The ordering rule

**A capture becomes a lane before it becomes a surface.** Do not surface first,
and do not do both at once. A surfaced capability whose only evidence is a
one-off screenshot is where 1.0's GPP writers were when WP-1B rewrote +547
lines of them. `rsop.py` is the precedent in the right order: twelve certified
scenarios first (WP-6, WP-9), then WI-030 surfaced it. It is the only layer
that has left the post-1.0 table.

For the capture-backed modules the facts are known. The expensive step is
turning a person's one-off observation into something the harness re-runs.

## WP-1 — Turn the six capture-backed modules into lanes

**The work.** For each of `security_template`, `object_security`,
`policy_families`, `script_policy`, `gpmc_interop` and `publication`: a
scenario in the remediation corpus, a candidate builder, a finalizer binding
through `oracle_evidence.py`, and a verdict. The measured facts become the
assertions; the lane re-runs them.

**Four of the six already have most of a lane.**

- `script_policy` has a round-trip result (R10): Windows consumed Studio's
  bundle and re-emitted it byte-identical, twice.
- `publication` composes two already-certified lanes.
- `object_security` has a `secedit /validate` oracle (R9), a command-line tool
  the harness can already drive.
- `policy_families` was the survey's item 1 because the existing WP-3 lane can
  be pointed at it by editing one file.

**Two are expensive.** `security_template`'s read direction and
`gpmc_interop`'s `is_gpmc_editable` reach their oracle only through GPMC
snap-ins with no cmdlet surface. They stay capture-backed unless someone builds
console-driver capabilities for them. That work belongs in
`windows-console-driver`, not this repo.

**Ruled 2026-10-07: neither is pursued.** Reading a GPME-authored
`GptTmpl.inf` is out of scope until a Security Settings import surface is
proposed, and `security_template` exits through its consumers instead.
`is_gpmc_editable` is deleted along with the rest of `gpmc_interop` except
`InteropIssue`.

**Order.** `policy_families` (one file edit against a green lane), then
`object_security` (R9 already proved the `secedit` oracle), then
`script_policy` and `publication`, then the two that depend on the console
driver.

**Acceptance.** A verdict per module, bound to the harness that produced it
(WI-045), with candidate hashes recorded (WI-025). The capability matrix moves
a module from `capture-backed` to `yes` only when a surface also exists (WP-3).

## WP-3 — Surfaces, and only behind evidence

**The work.** Wire each module WP-1 certifies to something an operator reaches:
an API endpoint, a browser panel or an export path. Follow the `rsop.py`
pattern: put the limitations in the response, and state in the surface itself
what the capability cannot answer.

**The gate.** A module reaches WP-3 only after WP-1 gives it a verdict. This is
the plan's review gate. Expect schedule pressure to push hardest here, because
surfaces are the visible half.

**WI-055 had to close before `object_security` was surfaced.**
`object_security.py` parses ACLs down to trustee SIDs and does not judge them:
an ACE granting Everyone full control passes validation silently. That was
harmless while the module was unsurfaced and becomes an operator-facing hazard
once a surface exists. WI-055 closed by ruling on 2026-09-07: ACL content is
deliberately not judged. The surface must say so in every response (the
`acl_content_is_not_judged` limit), so that a clean result does not read as
approval of the access granted.

## WP-4 — `folder_redirection`: a decision before a lane

R3 established that the policy lives in `fdeploy1.ini`, with an empty
`fdeploy.ini` marker, and that `folder_redirection.py` addresses neither. Like
`software_install`, the question was the module's scope, not its code.

**Ruled 2026-09-11: read target; writer deferred behind R12.**
[`scope-decision-2026-09-11-folder-redirection.md`](../docs/scope-decision-2026-09-11-folder-redirection.md)
records the ruling. The brief below is the argument it rests on.

### The read surface

`src/gpo_studio/fdeploy.py` is the read half: a strict UTF-16LE/BOM codec,
lossless parse, structural validation, review rendering, and a diff keyed on
`(folder GUID, principal)`. It is reachable at
`POST /api/folder-redirection/fdeploy`.

It is the first module in this plan to exit at a surface **without a lane
behind it**. No oracle has read this artifact in either direction. The banked
R3 capture does a lane's job: the reader is tested against bytes hash-bound to
what GPMC wrote.

Four limits ride in every response and in the module itself:

1. `Flags` is carried and no bit is named (WI-066).
2. One capture is one shape, so multi-folder and multi-principal documents are
   unmeasured.
3. Folder names come from the documented KNOWNFOLDERID table; only Documents is
   corroborated by a capture.
4. The surface is read-only. The writer is deferred behind WI-066/R12.

The parse reaches no `GPO`, because that field lands in `model.py` and two live
verdicts bind it. That is WI-068, filed against the batch that already owes
WI-063 through WI-065.

### The brief behind the ruling

[`scope-brief-2026-09-11-folder-redirection.md`](../docs/scope-brief-2026-09-11-folder-redirection.md)
assembled what the ruling needed, with no estate time: the decisive capture was
R3's and the demand figure R6's. The question was whether Folder Redirection is
a write target, a read target or out of scope, using the same demand evidence
that settled Software Installation.

Of the four reasons that ruled Software Installation out, two carry over and
two do not:

- **Carry over:** zero measured demand (0 of the same 26 GPOs), and a module
  modelling something no Windows tool emits.
- **Do not carry over:** Software Installation's capture was the most expensive
  unrun one in the survey, while this one is already taken. And `.aas` has no
  documented format and no oracle, while `fdeploy1.ini` is a 458-byte UTF-16LE
  INI whose oracle is `Backup-GPO`.

So the brief stopped at a recommendation (read target, write explicitly
deferred) and left the decision to the ruling.

The brief also ran the one measurement the survey asked for: an advanced policy
with three group rules yields **one** registry tuple, carrying neither the
group SIDs nor any of the four option flags. R3 shows Windows encoding both.
`to_registry_settings()` is not a Folder Redirection writer at any level of
detail.

`tests/test_folder_redirection_scope.py` pinned that measurement until
`folder_redirection.py` was deleted on 2026-10-07; the measurement is still in
the brief. The test still pins the banked capture, asserts that the decision
document exists and names its deferral, and checks that no module outside
`fdeploy.py` emits fdeploy bytes. A writer appearing before R12 turns it red.
