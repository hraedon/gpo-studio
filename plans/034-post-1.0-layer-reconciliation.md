## Why this plan exists, and why it could not have been written in August

[`direction-2026-08-06`](../docs/direction-2026-08-06-reconciliation-and-lab-handover.md)
Ruling 1 made reconciling the blind-built post-1.0 layers *the programme*, and
set the sequence: the oracle survey, **then a plan built from it**. The survey
landed on 2026-08-07. The plan did not, and the gap was not neglect — the survey
scoped seventeen modules on predictions, and eleven of its discriminators were
still unexecuted. A plan written then would have been a schedule of guesses.

Those eleven ran between 2026-09-02 and 2026-09-05. Nine of seventeen modules
now have oracle contact, five needed correction, one was confirmed correct, and
one had its *scope* invalidated rather than its code. Two of the five `(c)`
decisions have since been taken, and a third was taken in August. This plan is
therefore built on measurement where the survey had hypotheses, and on rulings
where it had open questions.

**What has not changed is the ruling that governs all of it.**
[`domain-layer-status.md`](../docs/domain-layer-status.md) still holds: these
layers are unproven drafts, the salvage value is structure rather than
behaviour, and a lane should be budgeted expecting to rewrite what it touches.
Nine modules having had oracle contact does not soften that — it confirms it.
Five of nine were wrong.

## The shape of the work, and the one rule that orders it

Every module in the first group is in the same position: **the facts are known
and the lane is not built.** That is a different and much better problem than the
survey faced, and it changes what the expensive step is. It is no longer *finding
out what Windows does* — it is making a person's one-off observation into
something the harness re-runs.

**The rule: a capture becomes a lane before it becomes a surface.** Not the
reverse, and not both at once. A surfaced capability whose evidence is a
screenshot from one afternoon is exactly the position 1.0's GPP writers were in
when WP-1B rewrote +547 lines of them. `rsop.py` is the worked precedent in the
right order — twelve certified scenarios first (WP-6, WP-9), then WI-030 surfaced
it — and it is the only layer that has ever left the post-1.0 table.

## WP-1 — Turn the six capture-backed modules into lanes

**The work.** For each of `security_template`, `object_security`,
`policy_families`, `script_policy`, `gpmc_interop`, `publication`: a scenario in
the remediation corpus, a candidate builder, a finalizer binding through
`oracle_evidence.py`, and a verdict. The measured facts become the *assertions*;
the lane is what re-runs them.

**Why this is cheaper than it looks.** Four of the six already have most of a
lane. `script_policy` has a round-trip result (R10) that is a lane in all but
name — Windows consumed Studio's bundle and re-emitted it byte-identical, twice.
`publication` composes two already-certified lanes. `object_security` has a
`secedit /validate` oracle (R9) that is a command-line tool the harness can
already drive. `policy_families` was the survey's item 1 precisely because the
existing WP-3 lane can be pointed at it by editing one file.

**Why two of the six are not cheap.** `security_template`'s read direction and
`gpmc_interop`'s `is_gpmc_editable` reach their oracle through GPMC snap-ins with
no cmdlet surface. These stay capture-backed unless someone builds console-driver
capabilities for them — which `windows-console-driver` now does routinely, and
which is the right home for that work rather than this repo.

**Ordering.** `policy_families` first (one file edit against a green lane), then
`object_security` (a `secedit` oracle already proven by R9), then `script_policy`
and `publication`, then the two console-driver-dependent ones.

**Acceptance.** A verdict per module, bound to the harness that produced it
(WI-045), with candidate hashes recorded (WI-025). The capability matrix moves
each from `capture-backed` to `yes` only when a surface also exists — see WP-3.

**Publication prerequisite, reviewed 2026-09-07:** the planner now explicitly
fails validation when a SYSVOL-targeted plan contains preserved CSE metadata
or files. The qualified Scripts rebackup demonstrates the previous omission:
its two INI files produced no publication step. This is a local completeness
refusal, not a Windows publication verdict.

The next plan-completeness lane can use native GroupPolicy/GPMC operations
and compare their observed state with the named plan steps.

**Plan-completeness probed 2026-09-07, and it found the gap on the AD side
rather than the file side.** One synthetic GPO (machine + user registry, a
computer-side Services preference, a user-side Drives preference) was exported
through `gpmc_backup_bundle`, imported with `Import-GPO` on LabMS01, and its
SYSVOL tree and directory object read back. The **file** half is complete: the
five files Windows produced are exactly the five the plan names, and
`Version=65537` — user half 1, machine half 1 — independently confirms the
packed-field fix R5/R8 landed, since the plan declared `version_half: "both"`.
The **directory** half is not: `gPCMachineExtensionNames` and
`gPCUserExtensionNames` each carry three pairs after import, and the planner has
no step that writes either, so a plan executed exactly as written yields a GPO
whose files are all correct and which applies nothing. Filed as
[WI-057](../docs/work-items.md#wi-057--a-publication-plan-writes-every-file-and-registers-no-extension);
the smaller `GPO.cmt`/description omission found alongside it, and correctly
attributed by a control run, is [WI-058](../docs/work-items.md#wi-058--a-gpos-description-has-no-publication-step).

**Both gaps are now closed in the planner** (`d15d8a6`): it emits an
`update_extension_lists` step per side carrying the measured value, sourced
from `export.py`'s vocabulary rather than restated, and a `write_gpo_comment`
step on the control run's condition. Content that cannot be honestly registered
is refused rather than guessed — an unverified GPP family, or a SYSVOL-only
target that cannot reach a directory attribute.

**This is a capture, not a lane, and the distinction is this plan's own rule.**
It ran twice with a control, on the frozen profile, but it has no candidate
builder, no finalizer, no hash binding and no verdict — so it does not retire
WP-1's `publication` item, and nothing here may be counted as qualification.
What it does is tell the lane what to assert.

**The lane now exists, and the fix is Windows-verified.**
`publication-completeness-20260907193221-2759` passed 21/21 on LabMS01 at
`362699c`: the plan's file set matched what Windows produced in both
directions, both extension-list attributes matched byte for byte, and the
packed GPT.INI version moved the declared half. The values the planner emits
are no longer only pinned by a test against a one-off observation — a
re-runnable lane re-earns them.

`publication.py` therefore leaves `capture-backed` and becomes **lane-backed
and unsurfaced**, which under `domain-layer-status.md` is one half of the exit
condition rather than the whole of it. What the lane does *not* establish is
worth being exact about: it measures the **plan**, not a publication. Nothing
in it writes to SYSVOL or AD on Studio's behalf, the operation allowlist is
still empty, and security filtering, links and WMI filters have no coverage
because `Import-GPO` restores settings rather than links. One GPO shape was
measured. See [the results](../docs/plan-033/publication-completeness-results.md).

**One cost worth recording, because it is the argument for batching.**
Touching `export.py` invalidated the Scripts metadata verdict that binds it,
and the honest repair was to re-run that lane
(`scripts-r10-20260907182809-4583`, 21/21, clean tree at `f8a2bbd`) rather than
re-hash its record. That is WI-048's ordering argument arriving on schedule: a
source change inside a bound file set costs a lane re-run, and a batch of such
changes costs one. Promotion of the
legacy script branch is separately blocked: its direct live-file mutation
conflicts with [the publication design](../docs/live-publication.md).
That branch must be replaced by supported API operations or explicitly retired
before a script-execution lane is built. The operation allowlist remains empty.

## WP-2 — Settle the five modules no oracle has read

**The work.** Run the survey's §3 and §4 discriminators. In priority order:

1. **`network_security.py` — the NetSecurity probe.** Two commands
   (`Get-Module -ListAvailable NetSecurity`, `Get-NetFirewallRule -PolicyStore`)
   on LabMS01. Still the cheapest unrun measurement in the whole survey, and it
   decides whether the firewall and IPsec halves get a re-runnable lane or join
   the manual queue. **Run this before booking any capture**, because the answer
   changes what a capture sitting should cover.
2. **`backup.py` — CSE-family breadth.** Count `GroupPolicyExtension` elements
   and extension-list GUID pairs in `Backup.xml` against Studio's `cse_metadata`.
   R6's census now supplies a real target vocabulary this can be measured
   against, which it did not have when the survey scoped it.

   **Discriminator corrected 2026-09-08 (WI-060):** Windows handler counts,
   registration pairs and unmodeled file counts are not interchangeable. The
   native captures include empty Registry handlers and fallback copy handlers.
   Imports now retain these separate observations plus exact native XML and
   every payload file's path/hash/size. The public import/report regression
   compares against independently read native captures. See
   [backup/report fidelity](../docs/plan-033/backup-report-fidelity.md) for the
   corpus and remaining family coverage.
3. **`report.py` — inventory against `Get-GPOReport -ReportType Xml`.** Surfaced
   code, small, and R8 says what a real GPO contains to compare against.

   **Partial correction 2026-09-07:** the plain-text report now counts every
   typed GPP family per scope. Native Drives, Services, and ScheduledTasksFull
   backup fixtures verify representative counts, and the runtime mapping has
   a completeness regression. This does not yet compare the full inventory
   against a fresh Windows report.

   **Source-inventory correction 2026-09-08 (WI-060):** unmodeled native
   extension values now appear in a retained source appendix, including the
   three startup commands previously absent from the Scripts report. This is
   historical observation, explicitly separate from editable current settings.
   Scripts and security ACL objects are still not typed report entries. Full
   report equivalence and the broader family lane remain open.
4. **`lifecycle.py` — the restore/scope-survival lane.** Medium, four boundary
   assertions. Its cross-domain half needs a second domain or forest trust and
   stays a named `(b)` residual.
5. **`artifact_store.py`** — a review, not a lane: extension policy, unverified
   `signer`, EICAR-only "malware scanning", hash-as-key dedupe with no
   provenance row. The survey classes it `(c)`; it is the one remaining `(c)`
   item and it needs no estate.

   **Settled 2026-09-07:** retain local integrity and policy checks, label the
   EICAR marker and secret heuristics accurately, refuse `.exe`/`.dll`
   publication until signer verification exists, and audit duplicate arrivals
   without replacing the canonical row. Publication eligibility is deliberately
   read-only and can differ from approval state. No Windows certification or
   script-payload delivery claim follows from this review. The
   [scope ruling](../docs/plan-033/artifact-store-scope.md) records the contract.

**Acceptance.** Each module ends with either a verdict, a scoped lane in the
queue with its blocker named, or an out-of-scope ruling. **None ends undescribed.**

## WP-3 — Surfaces, and only behind evidence

**The work.** Wire the modules WP-1 certifies to something an operator reaches —
API endpoint, browser panel, or export path — following the `rsop.py` pattern:
announce limitations in the response rather than burying them in docs, and state
in the surface itself what the capability cannot answer.

**The gate.** A module reaches WP-3 only after WP-1 gives it a verdict. This is
the plan's review gate restated, and it is the one place where schedule pressure
will push hardest, because the surfaces are the visible half.

**Known blocker to carry:** WI-055. `object_security.py` parses ACLs down to
trustee SIDs and does not judge them — an ACE granting Everyone full control
passes validation silently. That is harmless while the module is unsurfaced and
becomes an operator-facing hazard the moment WP-3 touches it. **WI-055 closes
before `object_security` is surfaced, not after.**

## WP-4 — `folder_redirection`, which needs a decision before it needs a lane

R3 established that the policy lives in `fdeploy1.ini` with an empty
`fdeploy.ini` marker, and that `folder_redirection.py` addresses neither. This
is the `software_install` shape: a module whose scope, not whose code, is the
question.

**The work is a decision first** — is Folder Redirection a write target, a read
target, or out of scope? — informed by the same demand evidence that settled
Software Installation. Unlike Software Installation, the artifact here is a
documented INI file that Studio could plausibly emit, so the answer is not
obviously "no". It is not, however, a lane until someone rules.

**Everything the ruling needs is assembled, 2026-09-11:**
[`scope-brief-2026-09-11-folder-redirection.md`](../docs/scope-brief-2026-09-11-folder-redirection.md).
No estate time; the decisive capture was R3's and the demand figure R6's. The
brief's contribution is the comparison the surface similarity hides: of the
four reasons that ruled Software Installation out, two carry over exactly (zero
measured demand — 0 of the same 26 GPOs; a module modelling something no
Windows tool emits) and **two do not**. Software Installation's capture was the
most expensive unrun one in the survey; this one is already taken. `.aas` has
no documented format and no oracle; `fdeploy1.ini` is a 458-byte UTF-16LE INI
and the oracle is `Backup-GPO`. So the two arguments that made that ruling easy
are absent here, and the brief stops at a recommendation — read target, with
write explicitly deferred — rather than taking the decision.

It also ran the one measurement the survey asked for and nobody had made: an
advanced policy with three group rules yields **one** registry tuple, carrying
neither the group SIDs nor any of the four option flags, both of which R3 shows
Windows encoding. `to_registry_settings()` is not a Folder Redirection writer
at any level of detail. `tests/test_folder_redirection_scope.py` pins that and
the banked capture, and fails when a ruling is acted on — so the brief cannot
outlive the code it describes.

## What this plan deliberately does not do

**It does not touch the 1.0 contract.** Everything here is post-1.0. The 1.0
capability matrix, its two-valued verification column, and the shipped release
are out of scope.

**It does not fork Plan 033.** The harness, the evidence contract,
`oracle_evidence.py`, the boundary matrix and the RSOP lanes stay Plan 033's.
This plan builds lanes *with* them.

**It does not schedule estate sessions.** Estate access is serial with
`windows-evidence-lab` by operator ruling, and harness-touching work must be
batched (WI-048's ordering argument) because every harness edit invalidates every
verdict bound to it. The batching decision belongs to whoever books the session.

What it does now provide is the price list that decision needs:
[`bound-source-cost.md`](../docs/plan-033/bound-source-cost.md) is generated
from the live verdicts and says, per file, how many lanes an edit costs. The
information was always complete and never readable -- it was spread across
twenty-one packs -- which is why "what does touching `model.py` cost?" was a
research task rather than a lookup. Three items filed against the next batch
(WI-063, WI-064, WI-065) were each deferred on exactly that number.

**It does not promise the layers are salvageable.** Five of the nine modules an
oracle has read needed correction, one had its scope invalidated, and one was
ruled out entirely. A WP-1 or WP-2 lane concluding "this module should be deleted"
is a **successful** outcome of this plan, not a failure of it.
